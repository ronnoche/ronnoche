Book Review: Ikigai

September 10, 2022
Book

Should you read Ikigai by Héctor García and Francesc Miralles? Yes, but with the right expectations. This book focuses more on the ikigai lifestyle than helping you directly find your ikigai (your reason for being). If you're searching for a guide to discovering your ikigai, this might not be the first book to pick up. However, if you are interested in living with intention, longevity, and balance, this book offers a lot of practical wisdom.

What stood out to me is its emphasis on everyday practices: mindful eating (the 80% rule), daily movement (walking, gardening, yoga, tai chi), connecting with nature, valuing community, and staying present.

The book also details a case study of Okinawa, Japan — a community known for having the world's highest life expectancy. Their way of life is deeply connected to ikigai: purposeful work, close friendships, and simple living.

Personally, I appreciated the book's exploration of ideas like logotherapy, flow, deep work, stoicism, mindfulness, and minimalism — concepts that resonate with me.

10 Rules of Ikigai (Lifestyle):

  1. Stay active; don't retire. Master your craft.
  2. Take it slow. Appreciate time and life.
  3. Don't fill your stomach. Follow the 80% rule.
  4. Surround yourself with good friends. Be intentional about relationships.
  5. Get in shape for your next birthday. Move your body every day.
  6. Smile. Stay cheerful. Humor is medicine.
  7. Reconnect with nature. Spend time outside.
  8. Give thanks. Practice gratitude daily.
  9. Live in the moment. Be present.
  10. Follow your ikigai — your reason for being.

Final Thoughts: Ikigai is a light, enjoyable read filled with reminders to slow down and align life with what truly matters. It's not a manual to find your purpose, but it is a gentle nudge toward a more intentional, fulfilling life.
